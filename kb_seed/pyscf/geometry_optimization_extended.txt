PySCF: geometry optimization, constraints and transition states (pyscf.geomopt)
Source: summarized from the PySCF user guide, pyscf.org/user/geomopt.html
This is a written summary, not the manual text. PySCF's own site cannot be
crawled by this app (pyscf.org/robots.txt disallows the scraper), so these
summaries ship with NexusQC instead.

## Backends and invocation

Optimization runs through one of two external libraries, geomeTRIC or berny,
exposed as pyscf.geomopt.geometric_solver and pyscf.geomopt.berny_solver.
Either call the solver's optimize() on a method object, or build an optimizer
from the method.

geomeTRIC convergence is governed by a set of thresholds covering energy
change, maximum and RMS gradient, and maximum and RMS displacement, all with
documented defaults.

## Constraints

geomeTRIC supports user-defined constraints supplied in a text file, whose path
is handed to PySCF. This is the route to frozen bonds, angles or dihedrals.

## Custom energies and gradients

Optimization can be driven by arbitrary energy and gradient functions rather
than a PySCF method, which is how corrections such as DFT-D3 get folded in. A
wrapper (as_pyscf_method) turns an energy-and-gradient function taking a Mole
object into something the solver accepts.

## Transition states

Two routes.

geomeTRIC performs a TS search when the transition flag is set in its input
configuration, with trust-region parameters available but not mandatory; the
defaults are tuned to maximize success rate rather than speed.

The qsdopt extension provides quadratic steepest descent TS optimization
(pyscf.qsdopt.qsd_optimizer), a second-order method that computes the Hessian
at intervals during the optimization. Its parameters cover how often the
numerical Hessian is re-evaluated (by default once at the start, then updated
by BFGS unless a trap region is approached), whether forward or central
differences are used, the maximum step norm, and gradient and displacement
convergence thresholds.

A real limitation: apart from the initial step, geomeTRIC cannot read an
analytical Hessian from the quantum chemistry code at runtime. PySCF can write
the initial Hessian to a temporary file and pass it in, and silently ignores
the request if the underlying method has no analytical Hessian.

## Excited states

Excited-state geometry optimization requires naming which state to optimize in
the excited-state method. States can flip during optimization, which shows up
as a convergence failure rather than a wrong answer. State-specific and
state-averaged CASSCF optimizations are both supported.

## Callbacks

A callback can run at every optimization step, which is the hook for per-step
population analysis or other monitoring. It can be passed to optimize() or
attached to the optimizer object.
