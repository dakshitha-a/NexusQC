PySCF: Green's function methods, GW, AGF2 and ppRPA
Source: summarized from the PySCF user guide, pyscf.org/user/gw.html, agf2.html, pprpa.html
This is a written summary, not the manual text. PySCF's own site cannot be
crawled by this app (pyscf.org/robots.txt disallows the scraper), so these
summaries ship with NexusQC instead.

Three related routes to charged and neutral excitations that do not go through
a wave-function expansion.

## GW (pyscf.gw, pyscf.pbc.gw)

Computes quasiparticle energies, that is ionization potentials and electron
affinities. PySCF implements G0W0: the self-energy is built from mean-field
orbitals and energies, so results depend on the starting point, which may be
Hartree-Fock or DFT.

All three implementations are full-frequency, differing in how the frequency
integration is done, selected with the freq_int keyword:

- Analytic continuation ("ac", the default). Cheapest scaling, supports
  spin-restricted and unrestricted references, supports frozen core orbitals.
  The continuation uses a Pade approximant by default, or a two-pole model.
- Contour deformation ("cd"). Slower but more robust, and specifically
  recommended for core and high-energy states. Restricted only.
- Exact ("exact"). Analytic integration requiring full diagonalization of the
  RPA matrix, so the worst scaling, but every orbital energy comes out without
  continuation error. Restricted only. By default it screens the Coulomb
  interaction with direct RPA, but any time-dependent mean-field theory (TDHF,
  TDDFT) can be substituted.

By default all orbital energies are computed, which costs more and can be less
accurate; a subset can be requested instead. The quasiparticle equation is
solved either by a Newton solver (default) or by linearization.

## AGF2 (pyscf.agf2)

Auxiliary second-order Green's function perturbation theory. It solves the
Dyson equation self-consistently using a second-order self-energy compressed
through its first two spectral moments, which turns the frequency dependence
into an effective single-particle Hamiltonian with auxiliary states. Loosely,
it behaves like an iterative approximate ADC(2).

Its distinguishing property: the entire spectrum of charged excitations comes
out at once, at no extra cost per excitation, because the full Green's function
is optimized rather than a few roots iterated. This makes full photoemission
spectral functions cheap to produce, and Dyson orbitals for individual
excitations are directly accessible.

Works with restricted or unrestricted references, with or without density
fitting. Correlation energies and one-particle static properties are available
alongside the spectrum. Calculations can be checkpointed and restarted, and the
module supports MPI as well as OpenMP.

Note that self-consistency on higher spectral moments is possible but degrades
results, so the first two are the default.

## ppRPA (pyscf.pprpa)

The particle-particle random phase approximation, originally a nuclear
many-body method. Its correlation energy is exact through second order in the
electron-electron interaction and is equivalent to ladder coupled cluster
doubles.

Excitation energies of an N-electron system are obtained as differences of
two-electron addition energies of the (N-2)-electron system through the
particle-particle channel, or equivalently as differences of two-electron
removal energies of the (N+2)-electron system through the hole-hole channel.
Having both channels available is what gives the method its flexibility.

It handles molecules and supercells on the same footing. The cost comes from
full diagonalization of the ppRPA matrix, which an active-space truncation in
the MO space reduces substantially without meaningful loss of accuracy. The
active-space approach works for restricted and unrestricted calculations; the
Davidson solver is restricted only.
