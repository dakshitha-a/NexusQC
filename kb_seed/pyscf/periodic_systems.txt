PySCF: periodic systems and crystals (pyscf.pbc)
Source: summarized from the PySCF user guide, pyscf.org/user/pbcgto.html, pbc-scf.html, pbc-df.html
This is a written summary, not the manual text. PySCF's own site cannot be
crawled by this app (pyscf.org/robots.txt disallows the scraper), so these
summaries ship with NexusQC instead.

## Building a crystal

Periodic systems use a Cell object (pbc.gto.Cell), built exactly like a
molecular Mole plus lattice vectors. The shortcut constructors pbc.gto.M() and
pyscf.M() work the same way. Cell.atom gives positions inside the unit cell.

Brillouin zone sampling is specified as a k-point mesh. Cell.make_kpts()
converts a mesh specification into the k-points in the physical units the SCF
classes expect (inverse bohr), which is the usual source of a silent unit
error if built by hand.

## Periodic SCF

pyscf.pbc.scf and pyscf.pbc.dft provide periodic Hartree-Fock and DFT with
Brillouin zone sampling. Their results feed the periodic post-HF and post-DFT
methods, which include periodic counterparts of MP2, coupled cluster, CI,
TDDFT, GW and electron-phonon coupling.

## Density fitting is mandatory here

Unlike molecular calculations, periodic SCF uses density fitting by default,
because the integrals are otherwise intractable. Three schemes differ in the
auxiliary basis:

- GDF, Gaussian density fitting, uses Gaussian-type orbitals as the auxiliary
  basis.
- FFTDF, fast Fourier transform density fitting, uses plane waves. This is the
  approach called Gaussian and plane wave (GPW) elsewhere. It is the default.
  Its auxiliary basis size is set by a kinetic energy cutoff, exposed as the
  mesh.
- MDF, mixed density fitting, uses both plane waves and Gaussians.

A range-separated scheme (RSDF) and a multigrid algorithm are also available
for reducing the cost of the Fock build in large systems.

DF error comes from the incompleteness of the finite auxiliary basis and is
reduced by enlarging it. Post-SCF calculations inherit whichever DF scheme the
SCF used.
