PySCF: orbital localization (pyscf.lo)
Source: summarized from the PySCF user guide, pyscf.org/user/lo.html
This is a written summary, not the manual text. PySCF's own site cannot be
crawled by this app (pyscf.org/robots.txt disallows the scraper), so these
summaries ship with NexusQC instead.

## Why

Canonical molecular orbitals are usually delocalized over the whole system. A
unitary rotation within an orbital subspace leaves the wave function unchanged
but can concentrate each orbital on an atom or a bond, which is what makes
orbitals interpretable and what makes manual active-space selection possible.

Two families of approach: project onto a predefined local set such as atomic or
pseudo-atomic orbitals, or optimize a cost function measuring locality. There
is no unambiguous locality criterion, hence several choices.

## The criteria

- Boys: minimizes the orbital spread, the second moment minus the square of the
  first. The periodic analogue is the maximally localized Wannier function.
- Pipek-Mezey: maximizes atomic population charges. It depends on which atomic
  orbitals the population analysis uses; Mulliken and meta-Lowdin based
  populations are both available. PM localization preserves the sigma/pi
  separation, which Boys does not.
- Intrinsic bond orbitals: a special case of Pipek-Mezey using intrinsic atomic
  orbitals for the population analysis.
- Edmiston-Ruedenberg: maximizes orbital Coulomb self-repulsion. More expensive
  than Boys or PM.
- Cholesky orbitals: obtained by pivoted Cholesky factorization of a
  density-like matrix. Non-iterative and unique except under degeneracy.
  Usually less localized than PM or Boys, which makes them an excellent
  starting guess for the iterative schemes rather than a final answer.

Intrinsic atomic orbital coefficients are also available directly, which is the
usual route to a population analysis expressed in the original AO basis.
