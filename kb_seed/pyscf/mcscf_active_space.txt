PySCF: choosing and controlling a CAS active space (pyscf.mcscf)
Source: summarized from the PySCF user guide, pyscf.org/user/mcscf.html
This is a written summary, not the manual text. PySCF's own site cannot be
crawled by this app (pyscf.org/robots.txt disallows the scraper), so these
summaries ship with NexusQC instead.

## CASCI versus CASSCF

Both build the wave function as a linear combination of all determinants
formable within a chosen set of active orbitals, which makes the CAS treatment
equivalent to FCI within that subset. In CASCI the orbitals are fixed and only
the CI coefficients are solved for. In CASSCF the orbitals are relaxed to
minimize the resulting CASCI energy, alternating CI steps with orbital updates.

MCSCF is what makes systems with near-degenerate orbitals, that is static
correlation, tractable. Transition-metal complexes are the standard case.

The FCI solver can be swapped for an external one, including DMRG, FCIQMC and
selected CI, and even MP2 or coupled cluster.

## Choosing the active space

Every strategy has two parts: state how many electrons and orbitals are active,
and optionally state which orbitals those are. The upstream advice is blunt and
worth repeating: visualize the chosen orbitals before committing to an
expensive run.

1. Specify nothing beyond counts. Orbitals are taken around the Fermi level.
   This is the default and, in most circumstances, a poor strategy that
   converges badly or not at all.
2. Start from better orbitals. Hartree-Fock orbitals are often bad for strongly
   correlated systems; DFT orbitals frequently make a better starting point.
3. Name the orbitals explicitly, as a list of MO indices passed to sort_mo.
   Note that sort_mo takes 1-BASED indices by default, unlike most other index
   arguments in the package. This pairs naturally with a visual inspection of
   localized orbitals.
4. Specify how many orbitals come from each symmetry group, useful for
   high-symmetry systems or where no chemically obvious choice exists. The
   related fcisolver.irrep_nelec sets electrons per irreducible representation.
   Both pair well with a cheaper preliminary calculation: MP2 or CISD natural
   orbitals with occupations near 2 can be frozen, and those with very small
   occupations dropped.
5. Select based on targeted atomic orbitals.

Orbitals can be frozen during orbital optimization, either as a count of lowest
orbitals or a list of 0-based indices, and those may be occupied, virtual or
active. The frozen keyword of the CASSCF optimizer is a different thing from
the frozen keyword of the FCI solver, which constrains orbitals to double
occupancy.

## Spin state

The FCI solver is spin adapted but the multiplicity is not directly
controllable; what is controllable is the spin projection Sz, through the
alpha and beta electron counts in the active space, which pins the multiplicity
in most cases. This is how a triplet is targeted from an RHF starting point.

When Sz is right but S^2 is wrong, from convergence onto another state or from
spin contamination, fix_spin_ biases the calculation toward the intended state
by an energy penalty. It can degrade both efficiency and accuracy and should be
left alone unless the calculation genuinely fails to find the target state.

## State averaging

When states lie close in energy their ordering can change during orbital
optimization, which shows up as CASSCF non-convergence. Optimizing the orbitals
for an average of states is the usual cure. state_average_ (note the trailing
underscore, marking a method that mutates its object) takes the state weights,
any normalized non-negative array, typically equal. A mixture of different spins
and point-group symmetries can be targeted.

## Restarting and analysis

Large runs should always specify a checkpoint file, which allows restarting
after an interruption and allows a converged small active space to seed a larger
one. Initial guess orbitals and CI coefficients can both be passed in.

The analyze() method reports the overlap between converged and initial guess
orbitals, the leading configurations and their weights, and spin densities
where applicable.

## Canonicalization and natural orbitals

The CAS wave function is invariant to orbital rotations within the inactive
core, active and virtual spaces. Inactive core and virtual orbitals are
canonicalized by default, which also yields orbital energies for them. Active
orbitals have no well-defined energy or occupation in that sense; requesting
the natural representation diagonalizes the one-body density matrix and gives
occupation numbers. Note that when natorb is set, the active orbitals may not
come out sorted by occupancy.
