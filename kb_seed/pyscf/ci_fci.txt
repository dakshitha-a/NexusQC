PySCF: configuration interaction, CISD and FCI (pyscf.ci, pyscf.fci)
Source: summarized from the PySCF user guide, pyscf.org/user/ci.html
This is a written summary, not the manual text. PySCF's own site cannot be
crawled by this app (pyscf.org/robots.txt disallows the scraper), so these
summaries ship with NexusQC instead.

## The two levels

FCI diagonalizes the many-electron Hamiltonian in the full space of
symmetry-appropriate Slater determinants. CISD keeps only determinants
differing from the Hartree-Fock reference by at most two occupied orbitals.

## CISD (pyscf.ci)

Available for restricted, unrestricted and generalized (spin-mixed) references,
giving RCISD, UCISD and GCISD. The ci.CISD(mf) constructor infers which from
the symmetry breaking in the mean field; the explicit classes live in cisd.py,
ucisd.py and gcisd.py.

Available properties: unrelaxed 1- and 2-electron reduced density matrices, in
the MO basis, and analytical nuclear gradients. The overlap of two CISD wave
functions can be evaluated, including wave functions from different
calculations.

Orbitals can be frozen by count (lowest-energy core) or by an explicit list of
0-based indices covering occupied and unoccupied orbitals alike.

Density fitting is not implemented for CISD.

## FCI (pyscf.fci)

A solver can be built from an SCF object, or from a set of orbitals plus an
electron count, or from arbitrary one- and two-electron integrals with a core
energy shift, which is how CASCI drives it.

Spin control matters and has two distinct handles. The solver takes mol.spin by
default. Passing nelec as an (n_alpha, n_beta) tuple to the kernel sets the
spin projection Sz explicitly. When the resulting wave function is
spin-contaminated, or when the initial guess has no overlap with the target
state, fci.addons.fix_spin_ applies an energy level shift penalizing states of
the wrong S^2. That penalty can introduce numerical problems of its own and can
break degeneracies that ought to be exact, so it is a remedy rather than a
default. Computing several roots is often the better way past a bad initial
guess.

Symmetry-adapted FCI solvers accept a target irreducible representation, which
also fixes the case where the plain solver converges to a state of different
symmetry than the Hartree-Fock reference.

Reduced density matrices are available to high order: spin-traced and
spin-resolved 1- and 2-particle matrices, and 3- and 4-particle matrices,
plus the corresponding transition density matrices between two CI vectors. Two
cautions carried by the upstream examples: make_dm123 and make_dm1234 return
quantities in an operator ordering that is NOT the density matrix, and must be
passed through reorder_dm123 / reorder_dm1234; and the 4-particle density
matrix is very slow to compute.
