PySCF: QM/MM electronic embedding (pyscf.qmmm)
Source: summarized from the PySCF user guide, pyscf.org/user/qmmm.html
This is a written summary, not the manual text. PySCF's own site cannot be
crawled by this app (pyscf.org/robots.txt disallows the scraper), so these
summaries ship with NexusQC instead.

## What it does

QM/MM splits a system into a quantum region and a classical region. PySCF's
qmmm module implements electronic embedding: the MM region is represented as
classical point charges that enter the QM Hamiltonian in the same way nuclear
charges do.

The total energy includes the ordinary QM energy, the interaction between the
QM nuclei and the MM charges, and the Coulomb interaction between the QM
electron density and the MM charges. It deliberately does NOT include the
MM-MM electrostatics, the MM force field energy, van der Waals terms, or any
bonded interaction across the QM/MM boundary. Those are the caller's problem.

A separate and more elaborate model, polarizable embedding, lives in
pyscf.solvent rather than here.

## How it is invoked

Decorate an SCF object with qmmm.mm_charge(), passing the coordinates and the
charges of the MM sites. Coordinates use the same units as mol.unit.

    mf = qmmm.mm_charge(scf.RHF(mol), coords, charges)
    mf.kernel()

Supported references are RHF, UHF, ROHF, RKS, UKS and ROKS. GHF and GKS are
not supported.

## Gradients

Analytic nuclear gradients including the background charges are available via
mf.nuc_grad_method(). This matters: calling grad.RHF(mf) directly on a
decorated object silently omits the background-charge contribution, so the
gradient is wrong rather than refused.

## Interactions with other features

- Post-SCF methods inherit the embedding automatically. Once mm_charge() has
  been applied, MP2, CCSD, MCSCF and so on all see the MM charges.
- With X2C relativistic corrections, apply mm_charge() after the X2C
  decoration. X2C gradients with MM charges are not supported.
- MM charges can be combined with second-order SCF and with solvation models.
- Geometry optimization can be run inside an MM charge environment.
