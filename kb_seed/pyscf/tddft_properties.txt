PySCF: TDDFT and TDHF properties (pyscf.tdscf)
Source: summarized from the PySCF user guide, pyscf.org/user/tddft.html
This is a written summary, not the manual text. PySCF's own site cannot be
crawled by this app (pyscf.org/robots.txt disallows the scraper), so these
summaries ship with NexusQC instead.

## What is solved

Frequency-domain linear response for Hartree-Fock (TDHF) and Kohn-Sham DFT
(TDDFT), giving excitation energies and transition properties. The response
equations are non-Hermitian and involve the same orbital Hessians that appear
in ground-state stability analysis.

Where the ground state is degenerate or has triplet instabilities, the solver
can be unstable. The Tamm-Dancoff approximation (TDA) removes that instability
by discarding the de-excitation block, at the cost of being a different, less
complete theory.

References may be restricted or unrestricted. Singlet excited states are
computed by default; triplets require setting the corresponding flag.

## Transition properties

- Oscillator strengths in both length and velocity gauge.
- Electric dipole, quadrupole and octupole transition moments, in both gauges.
- Magnetic dipole and quadrupole transition moments.
- Corrections to the oscillator strength from magnetic dipole and electric
  quadrupole terms, and optionally from magnetic quadrupole and electric
  octupole terms.
- Symmetry analysis of the excited states, which also reports oscillator
  strengths and dipole moments.

## Gradients and orbitals

Analytic nuclear gradients are available for both TDHF and TDDFT, defaulting to
the first excited state.

Natural transition orbitals come from a singular value decomposition of the
transition density matrix. The returned coefficients are in the AO basis and
are ordered occupied first, then virtual.
